The Cooking Timeline Guide

The secret to serving everything hot at once is working backwards. Start with when you want to eat and plan every step from there.

✦Start from serving time

Write your serving time at the bottom of a sheet of paper. Then list each dish with its cooking time and resting time, and work upwards. The dish that takes longest goes in first; quick dishes go in last.

✦A sample timeline

TimeTask
17:00Take the roast out of the fridge
17:45Heat the oven
18:00Roast in the oven (90 minutes)
18:45Potatoes into the oven
19:10Roast out to rest; turn the oven up for potatoes
19:15Green vegetables on; make the gravy
19:25Carve; warm plates
19:30Serve

Try the Cooking Timeline tool for your own times.

✦Use resting time well

Meat needs to rest after roasting so its juices settle. That 15–30 minutes is your window to finish roast potatoes at a higher heat, cook green vegetables and make the gravy from the pan juices.

✦Choose dishes that wait

  • Hold well: gratins, roasted root vegetables, braises, rice dishes, mash kept covered.
  • Serve at once: green vegetables, fried food, dressed salads, soufflés.

Try to have only one “serve at once” dish per meal.

✦Food safety while you wait

Keep hot food hot and cold food cold, and do not leave cooked food at room temperature for more than about two hours. Use a meat thermometer to check that poultry and minced meat are cooked through before resting.

✦When it runs late

Something always does. Keep a simple starter or snack in reserve, turn the oven down rather than off, and remember that a calm host matters more than a perfectly timed meal.
