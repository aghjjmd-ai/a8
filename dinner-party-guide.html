The Dinner Party Guide

A good dinner party is less about impressive cooking and more about planning. Get the menu, quantities and timing right, and you can enjoy your own table.

✦Build the menu around one star

Choose one dish to put real effort into, usually the main, and keep everything else simple. A slow-roasted shoulder with a crisp salad and good bread will always beat five complicated dishes cooked in a panic.

  • Pick at least two dishes you can finish the day before.
  • Avoid more than one dish that needs last-minute frying or searing.
  • Check oven space: two dishes at different temperatures can be a problem.

✦Quantities per adult

ItemPer adultNotes
Boneless meat or fish150–200 gRaw weight
Meat on the bone250–350 gRaw weight
Potatoes200–250 gLess if serving bread too
Rice or pasta (dry)75–100 gAs a main side
Green vegetables80–120 gPer vegetable side
Salad leaves30–40 gAs a side
Bread1–2 slicesMore if there is soup

The Dinner Quantity Planner scales these for your guest list.

✦Ask about dietary needs early

Ask when you invite, not on the day. Where you can, make sides that suit everyone, and keep a simple note of ingredients for any dish containing common allergens such as nuts, dairy, gluten, egg or shellfish.

✦Set the table the night before

Lay plates, cutlery, glasses and napkins the evening before. Forks on the left, knives and spoons on the right, worked from the outside in by course. A jug of water and a small vase of something seasonal is all the decoration a table needs.

✦Serve family-style

Bringing dishes to the table and letting guests help themselves is warmer, quicker and easier than plating for everyone. It also means people take what they like and the host sits down sooner.

✦Hosting, not cooking

When guests arrive, the cooking should be nearly done. Have a drink or snack ready, give yourself a clear five minutes to greet people and keep a clean tea towel and an empty dishwasher ready for the end of the evening.
